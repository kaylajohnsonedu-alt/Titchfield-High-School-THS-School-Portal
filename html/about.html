School History

Titchfield High School is a historic high school located at 22 Fort George Street in Port Antonio, Portland, Jamaica. Founded in January 1786, it stands as the fifth-oldest high school in Jamaica.

1786

Foundation

Originally established as a "free school" under the Titchfield Trust to provide education in reading, writing, arithmetic, Greek, and Latin (primarily for poor white children of the era, as no formal system existed for enslaved children).

1883–1886

Fort George Relocation

The Jamaica School Commission took over management from the trust in 1883 and later relocated the school into the historic Fort George military barracks—a site originally built in 1729 featuring 10-foot walls and cannons overlooking the bay.

1885

The Major Plant Era

Headmaster Major W.H. Plant shaped and transformed the institution into a prominent modern school.

1927

Transition to Modern THS

The school completed its transition into the unified Titchfield High School operating across grades 7 through 13.

Landmark Accomplishments

Titchfield High School's landmark accomplishments span over two centuries of pioneering education, national quiz championships, and historic athletic firsts in Jamaica.

Academic & Institutional Milestones

Fifth-Oldest High School in Jamaica (1786). Established in January 1786 under the Titchfield Trust, it is one of the Caribbean's oldest surviving secondary institutions. National Heritage Site Campus (1886). Relocated into the historic 18th-century Fort George military barracks in Port Antonio, operating on grounds protected by the Jamaica National Heritage Trust. National Academic Honors. Consistently maintains strong regional exam results (averaging 85% CSEC and 88% CAPE pass rates), producing top national CAPE students in Applied Mathematics and Electrical & Electronic Engineering Technology, as well as earning the Ministry of Education's Spirit of Care Award for overall academic and athletic excellence.

Academic & Institutional Milestones

2016 TVJ Schools' Challenge Quiz Champions. Defeated Campion College to become the first rural school in nearly 20 years—and only the 13th school ever—to win Jamaica's premier high school quiz competition. 2025 TVJ Schools' Challenge Quiz Champions. Captured their second national title by defeating Jamaica College 26–23 in the championship match.

Athletic Milestones

First Back-to-Back Girls' Champs Winners (1963–1964). Made history as the first school to win consecutive Inter-Secondary Schools Girls' Championships titles. Founding Member of the daCosta Cup (1950). Competed as one of the four original schools in the inaugural rural schoolboy football season and went on to reach three daCosta Cup finals (1964, 1982, and 2003). Eastern Champs Dominance. Captured the 2025 Eastern Athletics Championships and successfully defended the Eastern Champs boys' title in 2026, alongside winning national U-19 boys' table tennis titles.

Admissions & Enrolment

Admissions to Titchfield High School follow Jamaica's public secondary school entry pathways—covering Grade 7 placement via the Primary Exit Profile (PEP), Sixth Form (Grades 12–13) applications, and student transfers.

1. Grade 7 Entry (PEP Placement)

Application Route. Incoming first-form students are placed through the Ministry of Education, Skills, Youth & Information (MoESYI) Primary Exit Profile (PEP). Parents select Titchfield High School among their seven school choices on the Grade 6 PEP registration form. Registration Timeline. Following the release of PEP results in June, official registration for newly placed Grade 7 students typically takes place in late July (sorted alphabetically by surname/form group).

1. Grade 7 Entry (PEP Placement)

Standard Registration Documents. Per MoESYI Registration Guidelines, parents/guardians must present:

  • 👉 Completed school registration form and passport-sized photographs
  • 👉 Original Birth Certificate
  • 👉 Up-to-date Immunization Card
  • 👉 PEP placement/student report containing the child's Student Registration Number (SRN)

2. Sixth Form Entry (Grades 12–13 / CAPE)

Application Window. Sixth Form applications open immediately after CSEC results are released in mid-August and remain open for roughly one week (for example, mid-to-late August). How to Apply. Prospective Grade 12 students collect application forms from the school's main office (typically 8:30 AM – 2:00 PM during the application window) and attend a mandatory parent/student orientation meeting in the school auditorium.

2. Sixth Form Entry (Grades 12–13 / CAPE)

Required Documents. Applicants must submit:

  • 👉 Completed Sixth Form application form
  • 👉 Copy of CSEC examination results
  • 👉 1 passport-size photograph
Sixth Form Contact. Specific inquiries for Grade 12/13 placement are handled via thssixthform@titchfieldhigh.edu.jm

3. Student Transfers (Grades 7–11)

Step 1: Request Space. Parents submit a formal transfer request (along with the student's recent school reports and a character reference from their previous school) directly to Titchfield High School. Step 2: Ministry Approval. Once Titchfield issues an Acceptance Letter, the parent obtains a Release Letter from the student's current school (both listing the student's name and SRN) and submits them with a request letter addressed to the Permanent Secretary at the MoESYI Regional Office for final approval.

Community & People

The Titchfield High School community spans over 1,600 students, 100 faculty members, and a global network of more than 15,000 alumni.

Campus Leadership & Demographics

Current Leadership. The school is led by Principal Richard A. Thompson alongside Board Chairman Mrs. Fay Neufville. Student & Staff Body. Titchfield serves 1,600+ students across Grades 7–13 (ages 11–18), supported by a teaching faculty of roughly 101 educators. School Spirit & Identity. Students and staff rally behind the nicknames "Titch" and "1Titch", the cheers "Titchfield is the Greatest" and "Star of the East", and the school song "Follow Up! Follow Up!".

Student Life, Houses & Clubs

The Six Houses. Every student belongs to one of six houses named after influential past principals and benefactors: Brown, Chin, Geddes, Grossett, Plant, and Sherlock. Student Leadership & Clubs. Beyond sports, student life centers on active governance (Prefect Body, Head Students, and Student Council) and extracurriculars.

Extracurricular Activities

Academic & Cultural Societies. Debate, Drama, Music, STEM, and Environmental clubs. Publications & Traditions. Students produce the school newspaper, The Titchfield Peninsular, and participate in morning devotions, Founders' Day, Heritage Week, and annual talent shows. Wellness & Peer Support. Campus programs include peer mentorship, academic tutoring, and dedicated student mental health and wellness counseling.

Local Port Antonio Ties & Outreach

Historic Titchfield Peninsula. Situated on the scenic peninsula overlooking Port Antonio's twin harbors, the school has served as a social and educational anchor for Portland Parish since its founding as a free school in 1786. Community Health & Service. The school regularly opens its Fort George campus to Port Antonio residents for public health fairs—offering free medical checks, blood pressure screenings, and blood sugar testing—alongside student-led parish service projects. Honoring Local Educators. Campus landmarks celebrate long-serving community builders, such as the main driveway renamed in honor of former principal Lloyd O'Gilvie (who served from 1976 to 1992).

Global Alumni Network & PTA

Titchfield maintains a highly active Parent-Teacher Association (PTA) and an international alumni network—coordinated through the Titchfield High School Alumni Network—that funds scholarships, library upgrades, and athletic programs. THS Alumni Network (Jamaica). Founded in 1920 with over 5,000 members, leading local mentorship, infrastructure projects, and annual scholarship funds. International Chapters. Active diaspora branches operate in South Florida (which contributed over US$43,000 in 2025 toward scholarships and library upgrades), New York, and Ontario, Canada, alongside individual year-group associations (such as the Class of '83) and the Titchfield Past Students Sports Association (TPSSA).
